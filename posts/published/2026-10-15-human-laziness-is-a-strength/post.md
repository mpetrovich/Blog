---
title: Human laziness is a strength
subtitle: AI isn't lazy, which is why it keeps inventing ten ways to do the same thing.
date: 2026-10-15
topics: [ai, engineering]
---

Particularly in software development, the fact that AI is *not* lazy can cause problems. Human laziness, the productive kind, is often a strength.

Laziness forces us, instead of doing the same thing in a slightly different way by hand, to look for a more scalable, reusable, lower-effort way to do the same thing. Rather than doing it for the hundredth time, we create a tool, framework, or process that does it for us.

That instinct matters. Without it, teams end up with ten different ways of doing the same thing. Laziness pushes us to create one thing that does it consistently and easily, and then reuse that.

AI will happily generate another one-off solution. It does not get bored. It does not feel the friction of repeating nearly the same work again. So it can keep producing variations that work locally but fail to converge on a shared abstraction.

The engineer's "I don't want to do this again" is often the signal that something should be generalized. That signal is easy to lose when generation is cheap and tireless.

Good engineering laziness is not avoidance. It is impatience with unnecessary repetition. It asks: what would make this trivial next time?

Used well, that question is how we get better tools, clearer processes, and systems that stay coherent as they grow.
